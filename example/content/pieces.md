# How the pieces move

## King
The king moves one square in any direction. It may never move onto a square that is attacked by an opposing piece. Two kings can therefore never stand next to each other.

## Queen
The queen moves any number of squares in a straight line: along a rank, a file or a diagonal. It cannot jump over other pieces.

## Rook
The rook moves any number of squares along a rank or file. It cannot jump over other pieces. Together with the king it takes part in castling.

## Bishop
The bishop moves any number of squares diagonally and cannot jump over pieces. A bishop always stays on squares of the same colour, so each side starts with a light-squared and a dark-squared bishop.

## Knight
The knight moves in an L shape: two squares in one direction and then one square sideways. It is the only piece that can jump over other pieces. A knight always lands on a square of the other colour.

## Pawn
A pawn moves one square straight forward, and never backward. From its starting square it may move two squares forward, if both squares are empty. A pawn captures differently than it moves: one square diagonally forward. A pawn that is blocked by a piece directly in front of it cannot move forward.

## Capturing
You capture by moving a piece onto a square occupied by an opposing piece, which is then removed from the board. Capturing is never required, except when it is the only way to get out of check. The king can never be captured; the game ends with checkmate before that.
