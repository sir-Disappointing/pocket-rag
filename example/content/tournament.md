# Tournament rules

## Touch-move
<!-- keys: j'adoube -->
If you deliberately touch one of your own pieces, you must move it if it has a legal move. If you touch an opponent's piece, you must capture it if you can. Once you let go of a piece on a legal square, the move is made. To straighten a piece without having to move it, say "I adjust" (or "j'adoube") first.

## The chess clock
In timed games each player has their own clock. After making your move you press your clock with the same hand you moved with, which stops your time and starts your opponent's. If your time runs out you lose, unless the opponent cannot possibly checkmate you; then it is a draw.

## Illegal moves
If an illegal move is noticed, the position is restored to just before it and a legal move must be played. Under the FIDE Laws, the first completed illegal move gives the opponent extra time (two minutes in standard games, one minute in rapid and blitz). A second completed illegal move loses the game.

## Phones and electronic devices
Under the FIDE Laws you lose the game if your phone makes a sound during the game or you have it on you. Many tournaments only allow phones that are switched off and kept in a bag.

## Notation
In standard games you write down every move, usually in algebraic notation: the piece letter (K, Q, R, B, N; nothing for a pawn) followed by the square, such as Nf3 or e4. A capture gets an x (Bxe5), check a + and checkmate a #.
