# How a game ends

## Checkmate
Checkmate ends the game immediately: the king is in check and no legal move gets it out. The player who gives checkmate wins.

## Resigning
A player may resign at any moment, and the opponent wins. In tournaments a player usually resigns when the position is hopeless.

## Stalemate
Stalemate is a draw. It happens when the player to move has no legal move at all and their king is not in check. A player who is far ahead must take care not to stalemate the opponent by accident.

## Draw by agreement
Both players can agree to a draw. In a tournament you offer a draw right after making your move, before pressing the clock.

## Threefold repetition
A player can claim a draw when the same position appears three times with the same player to move and the same possible moves, including castling and en passant rights. The moves don't have to be in a row. After five repetitions the game is drawn automatically.

## The fifty-move rule
A player can claim a draw when the last fifty moves by each player were made without any pawn move and without any capture. After seventy-five such moves the game is drawn automatically.

## Insufficient material
The game is a draw when neither side can possibly checkmate, for example king against king, king and bishop against king, or king and knight against king.
